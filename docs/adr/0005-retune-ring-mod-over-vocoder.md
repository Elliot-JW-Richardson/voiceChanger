# Re-tune ring_mod using measured reference analysis, rather than pursue the vocoder further

Status: accepted

ADR 0004 built a real formant vocoder (Slices 32-36) to reproduce a reference track's character voice, on the theory that its fundamental "moves with the melody" and therefore needs a real-time pitch-*tracking* carrier, not a fixed one. Once wired into Magos and tested on real hardware, the vocoder destroyed voice intelligibility outright — confirmed by isolating it alone (no pitch shift, no bitcrush), which already produced buzz with no detectable speech. A follow-up attempt to mix a pitch-tracked sawtooth into the voice rather than replace it (`sawtooth_blend`) also failed on real hardware (extremely loud output, still no audible speech). Both were removed; Magos reverted to its original `ring_mod`-based chain, the last version confirmed to sound like a voice.

Rather than attempt a third vocoder-family redesign, the reference audio was re-analysed properly: a real pitch tracker (librosa's pYIN) run across the original reference track (isolating the character-voice verses specifically, separate from a clean sung chorus present in the same track) and a second, independent reference source (an unrelated spoken-word clip). Both show the fundamental is mostly **flat**, held at a low resting pitch (~45-58Hz), with brief rises at stressed syllables/phrase onsets before settling back — ordinary speech prosody pitched down onto a low register, not a continuously moving melody. This corrects ADR 0004's "moves with the melody" framing, which was likely an artifact of not separating the character-voice part of the track from other vocal content, and/or not using a robust monophonic pitch tracker capable of distinguishing genuine pitch movement from octave-jump tracking noise on distorted/growled syllables.

A mostly-flat fundamental is architecturally a much better fit for `ring_mod`'s fixed-frequency carrier than for a vocoder built specifically to track continuous pitch movement — the vocoder solved a problem the target didn't actually have, at the cost of real-time robustness against real speech it was never actually validated against.

Direct FFT analysis of a stable pitch region in the reference also found a specific, unexpected signature: the 2nd harmonic is louder than the fundamental itself. Testing against real recordings of the user's own voice found this reproduces naturally when `ring_mod`'s carrier frequency is set equal to the voice's own (post-pitch-shift) fundamental, rather than an unrelated "robotic buzz" frequency — multiplying a signal by a carrier at its own frequency pushes the fundamental's energy up into the 2nd harmonic. Magos's chain is re-tuned accordingly: a deeper pitch shift (landing the user's natural speaking pitch inside the measured ~45-58Hz target range) and a `ring_mod` frequency matched to that shifted fundamental, plus a high-shelf EQ boost to close a remaining brightness gap found during testing. See `voices/magos.yaml`'s header comment for the exact numbers and reasoning.

Every candidate chain was validated against real recordings of the user's own voice — comparing measured output (fundamental, spectral centroid, 85%-energy rolloff, 2nd-vs-1st-harmonic ratio) against the reference's own measured profile — before being shipped, not just checked against a synthetic test tone. That synthetic-tone-only validation gap is what let the vocoder's real-hardware failure go completely undetected through Slices 32-36's full unit test suite.

## Considered options

- **Attempt a third vocoder-family redesign** (e.g. more filter bands, a different envelope-follower design) — rejected for now: two real-hardware failures in a row from the vocoder family suggest the underlying premise (the target needs real-time pitch tracking) was wrong, not just the implementation details. Revisit only if a future need for genuine continuous pitch-tracking character voices arises.
- **Re-tune the existing, already-working `ring_mod` chain using measured data (chosen)** — smaller, evidence-grounded change to something already confirmed to sound like a voice, directly informed by what real analysis of the reference actually shows (a mostly-static low fundamental, not a moving melody), and validated against the user's own voice before shipping.
- **Reference audio and analysis scripts** are kept locally in `reference_audio/` (gitignored, not committed — copyrighted third-party material) rather than in the repository itself. Future re-analysis needs the reference re-supplied; the methodology (isolate character-voice segments via captions where available, track pitch with a robust monophonic tracker, compare FFT harmonic ratios, validate against real recordings of the target user's voice) is recorded here and in `voices/magos.yaml` instead.
